A garden on the roof

The committee proposes turning the flat roof of building B into a shared vegetable and herb garden, open to all residents from May.

The roof is already used for drying laundry, but most of it stands empty. A structural survey last autumn confirmed it can carry raised beds up to the weight listed below, as long as they sit along the load-bearing walls.

The association's reserve fund holds enough to cover this without raising monthly contributions. A local garden centre has offered a 10% discount on the beds if we order before March.

How the garden would run

  • Beds are shared by floor: each floor gets one bed and decides together what to grow.
  • Two beds stay open for herbs anyone can pick.
  • Volunteers take turns watering in summer, organised through the noticeboard.
  • The roof stays locked at night; residents can borrow a key from the caretaker.
Still to decide: whether children can use the roof without an adult, and who pays for water over the summer.
