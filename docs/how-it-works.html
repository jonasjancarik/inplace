How inplace works

What changes in your file when you edit on the page, how your agent hears about it, how comments work in both directions, and a few things worth knowing.

What happens to your file

The first time the page opens, inplace adds a data-id to each block and container that doesn't have one. The ids don't change how the page looks or prints, and existing ids are never renamed.

After that, the file only changes when you make a change on the page, and then only in the block you edited or the element you moved. If the document is in git, every edit shows up as a small, readable diff.

@@ garden-proposal.html @@-<h1>A garden on the roof</h1>+<h1 data-id="h1-1">A garden on the roof</h1>-<table aria-label="Estimated costs">+<table data-id="table-1" aria-label="Estimated costs"> …-<p data-id="p-4">Volunteers water in summer.</p>+<p data-id="p-4">Volunteers take turns watering in summer.</p>

How your agent hears about your edits

While it runs, inplace prints one line for every change made on the page, showing only the part that changed. The skill has your agent start inplace in the background and read these lines before it handles a comment or touches the file.

That way it notices a changed amount and fixes the totals and charts that depend on it, or a moved section and the “see the table below” that now points the wrong way. You can also just tell it to check your edits.

When you stop typing for a few seconds, inplace sums up what you changed, and an agent that’s following along picks it up straight away. The diagram below shows how that works when your agent waits for your pauses, as Claude Code and Codex do.

Comments

For anything bigger than a quick edit, select some text, or hover over a table, chart or row, and leave a comment on the page. Comments are kept outside your document, so they never end up in the HTML, and they survive a restart.

Your agent gets each one with the element’s data-id and the words you selected, so it finds the exact spot even after the wording has changed. Once it has dealt with a comment, the comment is marked done, with a note if there’s something you should know. If your agent’s own browser has a comment tool, as Claude’s and Codex’s do, you can use that too.

Your agent comments as well. When it notices something that’s yours to decide, such as a figure that doesn’t match your project’s data or a claim its sources don’t back up, it leaves a blue comment instead of changing your text. Answer it to tell the agent what to do, or dismiss it if nothing needs to change.

Good to know

Undo and review

Press ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z on Windows and Linux) to take back a move, an added or removed paragraph, or a heading change. Each block your agent changed has a blue bar beside it until you keep or undo the change; undoing it puts back what was there before, and your agent is told you didn’t want it. To read the whole document with every change in place, turn on “Show what changed” in the list of changes: your agent’s edits are marked like tracked changes, and editing pauses until you’re done.

Formatting stays plain HTML

Bold, italics, code and links are written as <strong>, <em>, <code> and <a>, so the document’s own styles decide how they look.

Editing at the same time

Edits to different paragraphs never get in each other’s way, and if you and your agent change different words of the same paragraph, both changes are kept. If you change the same words, the document keeps your agent’s version and yours waits under “edits set aside” at the bottom of the page. There you can put yours back, add it below, or copy it. Your agent is told about it too and can combine the two for you.

What to do on the page and what to ask for

On the page you can type, format, add and remove paragraphs, list items and rows, and reorder things among their neighbours. New sections or tables, moving something into another section, styling and charts are for a comment on the page.

Parts you don’t want edited by hand

Add data-locked to an element, such as a figure calculated from data, and it can’t be typed into or moved on the page. You can still comment on it.

Working with people nearby

Only your computer can open the document at first. Ask your agent to share it on your network and it gives you an address that works on your tablet or on a colleague’s laptop on the same Wi-Fi. There’s no login, so anyone on that network with the address can edit it. Ask your agent to keep it to this computer again when you’re done.
