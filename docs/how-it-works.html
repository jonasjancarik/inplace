How inplace works

What changes in your file when you edit on the page, how your agent hears about it, and a few things worth knowing.

What happens to your file

The first time the page opens, inplace adds a data-id to each block and container that doesn't have one. The ids don't change how the page looks or prints, and existing ids are never renamed.

After that, the file only changes when you make a change on the page, and then only in the block you edited or the element you moved. If the document is in git, every edit shows up as a small, readable diff.

@@ garden-proposal.html @@-<h1>A garden on the roof</h1>+<h1 data-id="h1-1">A garden on the roof</h1>-<table aria-label="Estimated costs">+<table data-id="table-1" aria-label="Estimated costs"> …-<p data-id="p-4">Volunteers water in summer.</p>+<p data-id="p-4">Volunteers take turns watering in summer.</p>

How your agent hears about your edits

While it runs, inplace prints one line for every change made on the page, showing only the part that changed. The skill has your agent start inplace in the background and read these lines before it handles a comment or touches the file.

That way it notices a changed amount and fixes the totals and charts that depend on it, or a moved section and the “see the table below” that now points the wrong way. You can also just tell it to check your edits.

When you stop typing for a few seconds, inplace sums up what you changed. Claude Code watches for that, or gets it as a message when it runs inplace as an MCP server, and catches up straight away. Codex can check in every few minutes, and any agent with a headless mode can be started by inplace itself.

16:34:18 edited p-1: "…proposes shared raised…" → "…proposes <strong>shared</strong> raised…"16:34:40 edited td-4: "€1,150" → "€1,350"16:34:52 changed p-3 from <p> to <h2>16:35:21 moved li-1 before li-3 (inside ul-1)16:36:02 added p-5 <p> after p-2: "A structural survey last autumn confirmed…"16:36:07 paused after 5 changes: edited p-1, edited td-4, changed p-3, moved li-1, added p-5

Good to know

Comments find the right element

Every block and table has a stable data-id. When you comment on an element in your agent’s browser, your agent gets that id and finds the exact spot in the file, even after the wording has changed.

Formatting stays plain HTML

Bold, italics, code and links are written as <strong>, <em>, <code> and <a>, so the document’s own styles decide how they look.

Editing at the same time

Edits to different blocks never get in each other’s way. If your agent rewrites the paragraph you’re typing in, your save is refused instead of overwriting its text, and what you typed goes to your clipboard.

What to do on the page and what to ask for

On the page you can type, format, add and remove paragraphs, list items and rows, and reorder things among their neighbours. New sections or tables, moving something into another section, styling and charts are for a comment.

Parts you don’t want edited by hand

Add data-locked to an element, such as a figure calculated from data, and it can’t be typed into or moved on the page. You can still comment on it.

Working with people nearby

Only your computer can open the document at first. Ask your agent to share it on your network and it gives you an address that works on your tablet or on a colleague’s laptop on the same Wi-Fi. There’s no login, so anyone on that network with the address can edit it. Ask your agent to keep it to this computer again when you’re done.
